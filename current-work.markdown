# Current Work

Our research focuses on developing practical forecasting systems for zoonotic diseases, with a primary emphasis on **Lassa fever in West Africa**. We work closely with public health agencies and international organizations to translate our research into actionable tools.

## Lassa Fever Research in West Africa

<img src="/assets/images/nyt.png" alt="Disease spillover risk diagram" class="float-right">

### Disease Overview
Lassa fever is a viral hemorrhagic fever caused by the Lassa virus (LASV), primarily transmitted from rodent species *Mastomys natalensis* to humans. The disease is endemic in West Africa, with Nigeria gathering the most detailed case surveillance data among affected countries.

### Key Characteristics
- **Viral spillover** occurs primarily in agricultural settings and homes
- **Limited human-to-human transmission** reduces epidemic potential
- **Extreme seasonal dynamics** in reservoir host populations drive disease patterns


## Sentinel Forecasting System

<img src="/assets/images/virus.png" alt="Disease spillover risk diagram">

We are developing an integrated real-time platform to forecast and manage infectious disease risk for public health agencies in West Africa. Model outputs are displayed online, alongside detailed background information on our modelling approaches.

### System Architecture

The platform includes:
- **Backend processing**: Automated data ingestion and model fitting
- **Data lake**: Climate, environmental, and socioeconomic data
- **Model pipeline**: Spatiotemporal forecasting models
- **Frontend interface**: User-friendly visualization and decision support tools

### Key Features
- **Automated updates**: Weekly/monthly data refresh and model retraining
- **Multi-scale predictions**: From local government areas to national levels
- **Uncertainty quantification**: Confidence intervals and validation statistics
- **Stakeholder integration**: Co-developed with end users for practical relevance

## Lassa Vaccination Strategies

<img src="/assets/images/vaccine.png" alt="Disease spillover risk diagram">

Although there are no vaccines currently available for Lassa Fever, several candidate vaccines are at an advanced stage of development. Determining how a future vaccine may be deployed to maximise public health benefits is a non-trivial task, given significant uncertainty around the disease's endemic area and rodent reservoir dynamics. In collaboration with CEPI, the Coalition for Epidemic Preparedness Innovations, we developed models to, firstly, assess the relative benefits of reactive (outbreak response) and pre-emptive (routine) vaccination campaigns for Lassa fever, and, secondly, to test the public health impact of different geographic scenarios of routine vaccination in Nigeria and across West Africa.

[**For full details explore our StoryMap on vaccination strategies →**](https://storymaps.arcgis.com/stories/a87af9084b3242bb837e8602396d07fd)

### System Components
- **Spatial nowcasting model**: Annual probability of Lassa fever occurrence
- **Temporal forecast model**: Weekly/monthly predicted cases (1-3 months ahead)
- **Data integration**: LGA/State-level case data with environmental variables
- **Visualization tools**: Maps, charts, and data stories for decision-makers
